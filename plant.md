# 🌿 Global Botanical Master Manual & AI Directive (plant.md)

This document serves as the foundational instruction manual and botanical knowledge reference for **Leafy AI**.
When Leafy analyzes any plant photograph or specimen, it references this standard to deliver an exhaustive, accurate diagnostic report covering taxonomy, ecosystem interactions, pest/beneficial insect attraction, and critical attention alerts.

---

## 📋 Mandatory Output Structure for Every Plant Analysis Report

Every AI visual report generated by Leafy MUST include the following components:

1. **Botanical Identification & Nomenclature**:
   - Common Name
   - Scientific / Latin Botanical Name
   - Plant Family & Regional / Traditional / Indian vernacular names
   - Habitat & Growth Habit (e.g., Climber, Succulent, Perennial Shrub, Deciduous Tree)

2. **🦋 Ecological Web: Insects, Pollinators & Species It Attracts**:
   - **Beneficial Insects & Pollinators**: Honeybees, stingless bees, butterflies, moths, sunbirds, hoverflies, ladybugs.
   - **Pests & Harmful Insects Attracted**: Aphids, white mealybugs, spider mites, thrips, caterpillars, fruit flies, fungus gnats, leafminers.
   - **Companion Organisms & Wildlife**: Earthworms, mycorrhizal fungi, garden birds, or domestic livestock attraction.

3. **⚠️ Immediate Attention Alerts (Soil, Water & Microclimate Diagnostic)**:
   - **Watering Status & Overwatering Diagnosis**: Symptoms of soggy soil, root rot, oedema, yellowing lower leaves vs. underwatering, crisp brown tips.
   - **Soil Condition & Potting Needs**: Needs repotting, rootbound signs, soil compaction, need for cocopeat/perlite, aerating topsoil, or adding fresh compost.
   - **Sunlight & Climate Calibration**: Sun scorch, leggy pale growth from low light, humidity deficits, or cold/frost shock.

4. **🌱 Care Benefits & Uses**:
   - Ayurvedic, herbal, culinary, therapeutic, and NASA air-cleansing qualities.

5. **🚨 Danger Alerts & Toxicity Warnings**:
   - Pet safety (toxic to dogs/cats via calcium oxalate crystals, saponins, solanine) and human contact cautions (milky sap, thorns, phototoxicity).

## 📚 Encyclopedic Plant Directory & Diagnostic Profiles

### 1. Money Plant (Golden Pothos / Devil's Ivy)
- **Scientific Name**: *Epipremnum aureum* | **Family**: Araceae
- **Regional Names**: Devil's Ivy, Ceylon Creeper, Feng Shui Wealth Vine
- **Reference Image**: `https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1596724805739-166e5f6e80b2?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80`
- **🦋 Species & Insects Attracted**:
  - *Beneficial*: Low pollinator attraction indoors; encourages beneficial predatory mites when kept clean.
  - *Pests Attracted*: Mealybugs (cottony white clumps in leaf axils), Spider Mites (fine webbing under leaves in dry indoor air), Fungus Gnats (if kept in soggy soil).
- **⚠️ Immediate Attention Triggers**:
  - *Overwatering*: Yellowing bottom leaves, black mushy stem nodes. Let top 2 inches dry out completely.
  - *Underwatering*: Drooping, limp, wrinkled leaves that perk up within an hour of bottom watering.
  - *Soil & Root Attention*: Needs porous, aerated soil (40% cocopeat, 40% garden loam, 20% vermicompost). If grown in water jars, change stagnant water every 7-10 days to prevent mosquito breeding.

### 2. Tulsi (Holy Basil / Sacred Basil)
- **Scientific Name**: *Ocimum tenuiflorum / Ocimum sanctum* | **Family**: Lamiaceae
- **Regional Names**: Krishna Tulsi (purple-black), Rama Tulsi (green), Vana Tulsi, Sacred Basil
- **Reference Image**: `https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1615485290382-441e4d049cb5?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80`
- **🦋 Species & Insects Attracted**:
  - *Beneficial*: Highly attractive to honeybees, stingless bees (*Trigona*), butterflies, and beneficial hoverflies during its flowering stage.
  - *Natural Repellent*: Strong eugenol aromatic oils naturally repel mosquitoes and flies.
  - *Pests Attracted*: Green caterpillars, lace bugs (causing pale bleached spots), aphids, and sooty mold following mealybug secretions.
- **⚠️ Immediate Attention Triggers**:
  - *Pruning Attention*: **Critical!** Regularly pinch off flowering tops (*manjari*); if seeds form, the plant believes its lifecycle is complete and will start drying out.
  - *Watering Attention*: Do NOT drown roots. Give morning sun water; never let water pool in saucer plates.
  - *Winter & Climate Attention*: Highly sensitive to cold below 12°C. Must receive 5-6 hours of direct sunshine.
  - *Soil Attention*: Needs aerated, well-draining soil. Scratch topsoil monthly and apply 2 spoons of vermicompost + neem cake powder.

### 3. Jasmine (Mogra / Arabian Jasmine)
- **Scientific Name**: *Jasminum sambac* | **Family**: Oleaceae
- **Regional Names**: Mogra, Gundu Malli, Chameli, Bela, Arabian Jasmine
- **Reference Image**: `https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1592150621744-aca64f48394a?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80`
- **🦋 Species & Insects Attracted**:
  - *Beneficial*: Night-flying sphinx moths, honeybees, carpenter bees, and hummingbirds drawn by intense nocturnal aroma.
  - *Pests Attracted*: Budworms (eat into immature flower buds), whiteflies, aphids on tender shoots, and red spider mites.
- **⚠️ Immediate Attention Triggers**:
  - *Bud Drop & No Bloom Alert*: Needs a minimum of 6 hours of hot, direct sunlight. If shaded, it produces only foliage with zero flowers.
  - *Pruning Attention*: Hard prune in late winter/early spring. Prune spent flower stems back to the nearest leaf node to force new flower shoots.
  - *Soil & Feeding Needs*: High-potassium fertilizer (mustard cake liquid or fermented banana peel brew) every 15 days during summer.

### 4. Henna (Mehndi / Egyptian Privet)
- **Scientific Name**: *Lawsonia inermis* | **Family**: Lythraceae
- **Regional Names**: Mehndi, Maruthani, Gorintaku, Camphire
- **Reference Image**: `https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1546842931-886c185b4c8c?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80`
- **🦋 Species & Insects Attracted**:
  - *Beneficial*: Small pollinating bees and butterflies attracted to creamy fragrant panicle flowers.
  - *Pests Attracted*: Extremely pest-resistant due to lawsone tannins; occasionally affected by black scale insects or grasshoppers.
- **⚠️ Immediate Attention Triggers**:
  - *Watering Attention*: Extremely drought tolerant. **Overwatering kills henna quickly.** Water only when soil is bone dry.
  - *Soil Attention*: Thrives in lean, sandy, or rocky garden soils with high drainage. Does not require rich compost.
  - *Sunlight*: Requires blazing full direct sun (7+ hours).

### 5. Oma Valli / Karpooravalli (Mexican Mint / Indian Borage)
- **Scientific Name**: *Coleus amboinicus / Plectranthus amboinicus* | **Family**: Lamiaceae
- **Regional Names**: Karpooravalli, Ajwain Leaf, Doddapatre, Panikoorka, Country Borage
- **Reference Image**: `https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1598880940371-c756e015fea1?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80`
- **🦋 Species & Insects Attracted**:
  - *Beneficial*: Blue banded bees and honeybees love its pale violet flower spikes.
  - *Pests Attracted*: Highly resilient due to pungent thymol oils, but susceptible to root nematodes and soft scale in stagnant wet soil.
- **⚠️ Immediate Attention Triggers**:
  - *Succulent Leaf Rot Alert*: Thick, velvety leaves store water. If leaves turn translucent or mushy, **stop watering immediately**.
  - *Soil & Drainage Attention*: Requires coarse sand and cocopeat mix in wide, shallow pots.
  - *Sunlight*: Prefers bright indirect light or mild morning sun. Intense noon desert sun scorches leaf tips.

### 6. Rose (Desi Gulab / Damask Rose)
- **Scientific Name**: *Rosa damascena / Rosa indica* | **Family**: Rosaceae
- **Regional Names**: Desi Gulab, Paneer Rose, Edward Rose
- **Reference Image**: `https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1518709268805-4e9042af9f23?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80`
- **🦋 Species & Insects Attracted**:
  - *Beneficial*: Honeybees, bumblebees, syrphid flies, and ladybugs.
  - *Pests Attracted*: Rose aphids (green cluster on tender bud stems), thrips (brown petals), caterpillars, spider mites, and leaf-cutting bees.
- **⚠️ Immediate Attention Triggers**:
  - *Fungal Black Spot & Mildew Alert*: Never splash water onto leaves at sunset. Water at the base only.
  - *Pruning Attention*: Angle cuts at 45 degrees 1/4 inch above outward-facing bud eyes. Cut deadwood and non-flowering blind shoots.
  - *Soil Feeding Needs*: Heavy feeder! Monthly aged cow dung compost, bone meal, and 1 tsp Epsom salt (magnesium sulfate) for vibrant petals.

### 7. Bitter Gourd (Pavakkai / Karela)
- **Scientific Name**: *Momordica charantia* | **Family**: Cucurbitaceae
- **Regional Names**: Karela, Pavakkai, Kakarakaya, Balsam Pear
- **Reference Image**: `https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1584308666744-24d5c474f2ae?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80`
- **🦋 Species & Insects Attracted**:
  - *Beneficial*: Honeybees and native solitary bees essential for pollinating female flowers (flowers with miniature baby gourds at the base).
  - *Pests Attracted*: **Fruit fly (*Bactrocera cucurbitae*)**—the #1 menace that punctures tender gourds causing yellow rot and worms; red pumpkin beetles, aphids.
- **⚠️ Immediate Attention Triggers**:
  - *Fruit Protection*: Cover baby fertilized gourds with perforated paper bags or mosquito mesh bags.
  - *Trellis & Space*: Must have vertical netting or bamboo trellises; allowing it to crawl on damp ground causes fungal rot.
  - *Watering*: Consistent watering daily. Erratic moisture results in deformed, stunted bitter gourds.

### 8. Tomato
- **Scientific Name**: *Solanum lycopersicum* | **Family**: Solanaceae
- **Regional Names**: Tamatar, Thakkali, Country Tomato
- **Reference Image**: `https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1592841200221-a6898f307baa?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80`
- **🦋 Species & Insects Attracted**:
  - *Beneficial*: Bumblebees and carpenter bees perform **buzz pollination** to release pollen from flowers.
  - *Pests Attracted*: Hornworms, whiteflies (vectors for leaf curl virus), leafminers (winding white trails inside leaves), spider mites, fruit borers.
- **⚠️ Immediate Attention Triggers**:
  - *Blossom End Rot*: Black sunken patches on the bottom of green tomatoes mean **calcium deficiency caused by uneven watering**. Water on a strict morning schedule.
  - *Pruning Suckers*: Pinch off auxiliary suckers growing in the 45° crotch between branches to redirect energy into fruit clusters.
  - *Soil & Staking*: Must be staked early with bamboo canes. Add crushed eggshells or dolomitic lime to potting soil.

### 9. Betel Leaf (Paan / Vetrillai)
- **Scientific Name**: *Piper betle* | **Family**: Piperaceae
- **Regional Names**: Paan, Vetrillai, Tamalapaku, Vettila, Nagavalli
- **Reference Image**: `https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1615485290382-441e4d049cb5?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80`
- **🦋 Species & Insects Attracted**:
  - *Beneficial*: Micro-wasps, beneficial predatory bugs.
  - *Pests Attracted*: Scales, red spider mites, mealybugs, and bacterial leaf spot bugs.
- **⚠️ Immediate Attention Triggers**:
  - *Sunburn Warning*: Cannot tolerate direct afternoon scorching sun. Needs dappled shade or green net filter.
  - *Moisture & Humidity*: Mist foliage regularly; loves 70% humidity.
  - *Support Attention*: Needs a moist coir pole or brick wall for aerial roots to cling to.

### 10. Curry Leaf Tree (Kariveppilai / Kadi Patta)
- **Scientific Name**: *Murraya koenigii* | **Family**: Rutaceae
- **Regional Names**: Kariveppilai, Kadi Patta, Meetha Neem, Karivepaaku
- **Reference Image**: `https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1628088062854-d1870b4553da?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80`
- **🦋 Species & Insects Attracted**:
  - *Beneficial*: Common Mormon butterfly (*Papilio polytes*) caterpillars feed on leaves; small bees on blossoms.
  - *Pests Attracted*: Citrus psyllids, brown soft scale, black sooty mold on sticky sap.
- **⚠️ Immediate Attention Triggers**:
  - *Pruning*: Pluck whole petioles (stalks), never individual leaflets. Prune main tip to branch into bushy growth.
  - *Miracle Soil Booster*: Water with diluted sour buttermilk (1:10 with water) every 15 days for deep green leaves.
  - *Winter Dormancy*: Leaves naturally turn yellow and drop below 15°C. Cut back watering until spring warmth arrives.

### 11. Neem Tree
- **Scientific Name**: *Azadirachta indica* | **Family**: Meliaceae
- **Regional Names**: Veppam, Margosa, Indian Lilac, Nimba
- **Reference Image**: `https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1615485290382-441e4d049cb5?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80`
- **🦋 Species & Insects Attracted**:
  - *Beneficial*: Bees feed on sweet fragrant white spring flowers; birds feed on yellow neem drupe fruits and disperse seeds.
  - *Pests Attracted*: Highly resistant due to natural azadirachtin; occasionally affected by tea mosquito bugs.
- **⚠️ Immediate Attention Triggers**:
  - *Root Space*: Aggressive taproot system. If grown in pots, repot into large 50L drums every 2 years or plant directly in soil.
  - *Watering*: Extremely drought hardy; never overwater.

### 12. Aloe Vera
- **Scientific Name**: *Aloe barbadensis miller* | **Family**: Asphodelaceae
- **Regional Names**: Kattralai, Ghritkumari, Kalabanda
- **Reference Image**: `https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1596547609652-9cf5d8d76921?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80`
- **🦋 Species & Insects Attracted**:
  - *Beneficial*: Sunbirds and bees visit tall yellow-orange flower spikes.
  - *Pests Attracted*: Aloe mites (gall-forming), mealybugs deep in leaf crown crevices.
- **⚠️ Immediate Attention Triggers**:
  - *Overwatering & Root Rot*: Mushy, translucent or brown base indicates root rot. Remove from soil, cut rotten roots, and let dry for 3 days before repotting.
  - *Watering Rule*: "Soak and Dry"—water thoroughly, then wait until soil is 100% dry (every 2-3 weeks).

### 13. Hibiscus (Sembaruthi / Shoe Flower)
- **Scientific Name**: *Hibiscus rosa-sinensis* | **Family**: Malvaceae
- **Regional Names**: Sembaruthi, Gudhal, Mandaram, China Rose
- **Reference Image**: `https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1550950158-d0d960dff51b?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80`
- **🦋 Species & Insects Attracted**:
  - *Beneficial*: Sunbirds, hummingbirds, butterflies, and bumblebees drawn to nectar tubes.
  - *Pests Attracted*: **White cottony mealybugs** (nestle in flower bud bases and leaf axils), aphids, yellowing spider mites.
- **⚠️ Immediate Attention Triggers**:
  - *Bud Drop Alert*: Sudden drying out, excess heat shock, or mealybug infestation causes unopened buds to drop off.
  - *Organic Remedy*: Spray 5ml cold-pressed neem oil + 2ml liquid soap per liter of water weekly.
  - *Feeding*: High potassium feeder (wood ash or banana peel compost) needed for continuous blooms.

### 14. Snake Plant (Sansevieria)
- **Scientific Name**: *Dracaena trifasciata* | **Family**: Asparagaceae
- **Regional Names**: Mother-in-Law's Tongue, Saint George's Sword
- **Reference Image**: `https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1593482892290-f54927ae1bf6?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80`
- **🦋 Species & Insects Attracted**:
  - *Beneficial*: Night-blooming fragrant floral stalks attract night moths.
  - *Pests Attracted*: Very few pests; occasional mealybugs at soil line.
- **⚠️ Immediate Attention Triggers**:
  - *Overwatering*: The only way to kill it. If leaves turn soft and yellow at the base, stop watering and check roots.
  - *Watering Frequency*: Only once a month in winter; once every 2-3 weeks in summer.

### 15. Monstera Deliciosa (Swiss Cheese Plant)
- **Scientific Name**: *Monstera deliciosa* | **Family**: Araceae
- **Regional Names**: Split-Leaf Philodendron, Mexican Breadfruit
- **Reference Image**: `https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1614594975525-e45190c55d0b?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80`
- **🦋 Species & Insects Attracted**:
  - *Beneficial*: Pollinated in natural tropical habitats by nitidulid beetles.
  - *Pests Attracted*: Thrips, spider mites on underside of giant leaves, scale insects on stems.
- **⚠️ Immediate Attention Triggers**:
  - *No Splits (Fenestrations)*: Immature leaves or lack of adequate bright indirect sunlight. Move closer to window.
  - *Brown Crispy Leaf Edges*: Low humidity or chlorinated tap water; use filtered/rainwater and mist regularly.
  - *Aerial Roots*: Never cut them; guide them into the pot soil or onto a damp moss pole.
